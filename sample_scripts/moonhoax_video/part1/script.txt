BEATS (VO text, read exactly):
1. August 1835. New York City is reading the strangest headline ever printed, there is life on the Moon. And everyone believes it.
2. The story comes from the New York Sun, a tiny penny paper, and a reporter named Richard Adams Locke, who claims it's all genuine science.
3. According to the Sun, the famous astronomer Sir John Herschel has built a giant new telescope, powerful enough to watch the Moon like a nature documentary.
4. Day after day, the paper prints his discoveries, lunar forests, sapphire rivers, and then, the creatures. Horned, bat-winged, moon men.
5. The articles describe the bat-people building temples, unicorns grazing by craters, and beavers that walk upright and live in huts.
6. New York loses its mind. The Sun becomes the best-selling newspaper on the planet, and every rival paper scrambles to reprint the moon news.
7. Excited readers even start raising money, some want to send missionaries, to civilize the moon people.
8. But one rival editor starts asking a dangerous question. Where exactly is this famous report, and why has nobody else seen the telescope?
