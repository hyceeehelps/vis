BEATS:
1. In the summer of 1858, London smelled so bad that people believed the air itself could kill.
2. By then, two and a half million Londoners flushed everything into the Thames, and the river had quietly become an open sewer.
3. Flush toilets were the new fashion, so more waste poured in every day. Then June arrived, with a brutal heatwave.
4. The river baked and bubbled. A brown fog of stench rolled over the city, and newspapers gave it a name: the Great Stink.
5. Ordinary people covered their noses and carried on. But the brand-new Houses of Parliament sat right on the riverbank, and there was no escape.
6. Desperate officials draped the windows in curtains soaked with lime. Some MPs threatened to abandon the building entirely.
7. Even Queen Victoria tried a pleasure cruise on the Thames. Legend says she retreated within minutes.
8. The government of the largest empire on Earth, defeated by a smell. But the Great Stink was about to do what decades of debate never could.
