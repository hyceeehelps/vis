BEATS:
1. July 1858. The Thames is an open sewer, Parliament is choking, and the Queen just fled her own boat. London must act, or run.
2. So the government hands the job to a quiet engineer named Joseph Bazalgette, and tells him to make the stink disappear.
3. His plan is outrageous. Intercept every sewer before it reaches the Thames, and carry the waste miles downstream, through brand-new tunnels.
4. Eighty-two miles of brick-lined sewers, dug entirely by hand, with more than three hundred million bricks.
5. For sixteen years, London is one giant construction site, streets ripped open, the biggest engineering project the city has ever seen.
6. And when it finally opens, the miracle happens. The stink fades, and the cholera outbreaks that killed tens of thousands simply stop.
7. Bazalgette's sewers still run under London today, more than a hundred and sixty years later, carrying everything the city flushes.
8. So if London now smells like rain, and not a toilet, thank the man who buried the problem. And yes, this actually happened.
