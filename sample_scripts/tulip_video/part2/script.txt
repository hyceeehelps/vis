BEATS:
1. Nobody bids. Within days, that silence spreads from one auction room in Haarlem to every tavern in Holland.
2. Prices do not dip, they fall off a cliff. Buyers vanish, sellers panic, and everyone wants out of a promise they happily made weeks earlier.
3. The same bulb that cost a mansion in January is worth less than an onion by spring. Some say prices dropped ninety-nine percent.
4. The contracts were the trap. Thousands of tavern IOUs, signed over drinks, now demand real money that buyers simply refuse to pay.
5. Ordinary people are ruined on paper: the weaver who traded his loom, the family that promised away their house, the sailor still in jail.
6. The government never bails anyone out. Courts mostly refuse to enforce the tavern contracts, and the wind trade quietly evaporates.
7. Modern historians argue the crash hurt fewer people than legend says, yet tulip mania remains the byword for every bubble since.
8. So the world's first famous bubble bloomed, popped, and taught everyone nothing. And yes, this actually happened.
