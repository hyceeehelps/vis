BEATS (VO text, read exactly):
1. Holland, 1636. One single flower bulb sells for the price of a mansion. And somehow, everyone thinks that makes sense.
2. Tulips arrive from Turkey as a luxury for the rich. The rarer the colors, the more status a garden brings.
3. The most desired are "broken" tulips, petals with wild flame stripes, caused, it turns out, by a virus. Nobody knows that yet.
4. Weavers, bakers, chimney sweeps, everyone starts buying. By 1636, people are trading bulbs they will never even see, in taverns, over drinks.
5. This is the "wind trade", buying and selling pieces of paper, promises for a flower still in the ground. Prices double in weeks, then in days.
6. One rare bulb, the Semper Augustus, reportedly sells for five and a half thousand guilders, enough to buy a grand house on Amsterdam's finest canal.
7. One sailor famously eats a priceless bulb with his herring, thinking it is an onion. It lands him in jail, while the market hits absolute madness.
8. Everyone is rich on paper. But in February 1637, at one ordinary auction, the auctioneer opens the bidding, and the room goes silent. Nobody bids.
